  Three design choices worth recording, because they are the ones a future session is most
  likely to want to undo:

  1. **Staleness is content-addressed, not commit-based.** Commit-range staleness breaks on
     uncommitted edits (the normal state during an agent session), on rebases and squashes,
     on cherry-picks across the many long-lived branches here (`main`, `ltuSP`, `map`,
     `pge_paper`, `DESxACT`, …), and on reverts. `verified_at_commit` is kept only as
     human-readable context.
  2. **Notebook outputs are excluded from the digest.** `kb` hashes only `.ipynb` source
     cells. Without this, the large notebooks in `notebooks/xDESI/` (1.8–3.9 MB of stored
     output) would mark their documents stale on every execution, the gate would be bypassed
     routinely, and the whole mechanism would decay into noise.
  3. **`.gitignore` was changed from `.claude/` to `.claude/*` plus negations** for
     `agents/`, `commands/`, `hooks/` and `settings.json`. Git does not descend into an
     ignored *directory*, so negations under `.claude/` would never have been evaluated.
     Without this the agent system would not travel with clones — which would defeat the
     laptop/cluster workflow this system exists to serve.
